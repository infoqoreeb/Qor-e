ORIGIN

I Did Not Enter Book Marketing Because It Was Trendy. I Entered Because the Industry Was Broken.

My name is Qore'eb. I am a book marketing specialist who builds data-driven, multi-channel marketing systems for authors across every genre in publishing. I operate exclusively through Gmail, ensuring every strategy, every decision, and every result is permanently documented and organized for future reference.

The Problem That Built This Practice

Several years ago, I watched an author I deeply respected publish a book that I knew, with absolute certainty, was extraordinary. The writing was masterful. The story was compelling. The editing was flawless. The cover was stunning. By every creative metric, this book deserved to be a bestseller.

It sold 87 copies in its first year.

Not because the book was bad. Because the marketing was nonexistent. The author had done everything right on the creative side and absolutely nothing on the commercial side. They had published into a void and assumed that quality alone would generate visibility. It did not. It never does.

That moment crystallized something for me. The publishing industry has a massive, systemic problem: it produces extraordinary books and then abandons them to an algorithm that does not care about quality. The algorithm cares about sales velocity, keyword relevance, conversion rates, and engagement metrics. Authors who do not understand these mechanics are essentially invisible, regardless of how brilliant their work is.

I decided to become the bridge between brilliant books and the readers who need them.

I spent the next several years obsessively studying every dimension of book marketing. Amazon's A9 and A10 algorithms. Facebook and Instagram ad auction mechanics. BookBub's selection criteria. Email deliverability and segmentation. Content distribution models. Keyword research methodologies. Category optimization strategies. Launch timing dynamics. Reader psychology across dozens of genres. Book mockup design principles. Book trailer production techniques. Goodreads Listopia ranking algorithms. Book club acquisition strategies.

I did not just learn the theory. I applied it. Campaign after campaign. Genre after genre. Author after author. I tested, measured, failed, adjusted, and refined until I had a repeatable, scalable framework that consistently produced measurable results. That framework is what I deploy for every author who works with me today.

PHILOSOPHY

The Three Principles That Govern Everything I Do

01

DATA IS THE ONLY AUTHORITY

In book marketing, opinions are worthless. "I think this cover will perform well" is not a strategy. "I feel like this audience will respond" is not a plan. The only authority I recognize is data. Every decision I make, from keyword selection to ad creative to email send times to mockup design to trailer scripting to Listopia targeting, is informed by measurable data points. I track over 30 distinct metrics across every campaign I run, and I use that data to make real-time adjustments that maximize performance. If the data says a strategy is working, I scale it aggressively. If the data says it is failing, I terminate it immediately, regardless of how much I personally liked the idea. My ego does not run campaigns. The numbers do.

02

TRANSPARENCY IS NON-NEGOTIABLE

The book marketing industry has a trust problem. Authors have been burned by marketers who overpromise, underdeliver, and then hide behind vague reports and confusing dashboards. I refuse to operate that way. Every campaign I run is fully transparent. You will see exactly where your money is being spent. You will receive detailed weekly and monthly reports in your Gmail inbox breaking down every metric. And because all of our communication happens over Gmail, you will have a permanent, searchable, organized record of every strategic decision we have made together. No hidden fees. No surprise charges. No "trust me, it is working" without proof.

03

SYSTEMS BEAT TACTICS EVERY TIME

A tactic is a single action: running an ad, posting content, sending an email, creating a mockup. A system is an interconnected architecture where every action feeds into every other action, creating compounding results over time. Most book marketers sell tactics. I build systems. When I run your Amazon Ads, they are not operating in isolation. They are feeding data into your keyword strategy, which informs your KDP optimization, which improves your organic ranking, which reduces your ad costs, which frees up budget for your Facebook campaigns, which drives traffic to your landing page, which grows your email list, which powers your next launch. Everything is connected. Everything compounds.

The Strategic Advantage of Gmail-Only Communication

I get this question frequently: "Why do you only communicate through Gmail?"

The answer is not that I am antisocial. It is that Gmail is objectively superior for the type of work I do, and I have structured my entire practice around it for very specific, strategic reasons:

Permanent Documentation and Organization

Every strategic discussion, every campaign adjustment, every piece of feedback, every approval, every performance report is preserved in your Gmail thread. Six months from now, if you want to review why we made a specific decision about your ad targeting, your launch timeline, your mockup design, or your Listopia strategy, it is right there in your inbox. Organized. Searchable. Timestamped.

Precision of Thought

Written communication forces precision. When I write you a Gmail message explaining a campaign strategy, I cannot rely on tone of voice or hand gestures to fill in the gaps. I have to articulate the strategy clearly, completely, and unambiguously. This results in better communication, fewer misunderstandings, and more effective execution.

Asynchronous Efficiency

Authors are busy. Many of my clients are in different time zones. Gmail lets us communicate efficiently on our own schedules. I respond within 24 hours, usually within a few hours, and you can read and respond whenever it is convenient for you. No scheduling conflicts. No missed appointments. No wasted time.

Accountability and Record-Keeping

When commitments are made in writing, they are binding. If I tell you in a Gmail message that I will have your ad campaigns live by Thursday, that commitment is documented. If you approve a strategy in a Gmail message, that approval is on record. This level of accountability protects both of us.
